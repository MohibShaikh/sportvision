# Design context

## Target audience

The owner has not pinned this down. The working assumption covers both likely
readings. **Someone who has a clip and a question about it.** Two overlapping
groups in practice.

- a coach, player, or analyst with phone or sideline footage, non-technical,
  who wants to see who had the ball and walk away with an annotated video;
- an engineer running the app to judge whether the `sportvision` library is
  worth building on.

The first group gets plain-language figures up front. The second gets per-frame
evidence and the raw JSON report, both reachable without leaving the page.

Revisit this section if the owner settles on one group.

## Use cases

1. Upload a short clip, get player tracking and team assignment.
2. Correct the model where it got teams, ball position, or possession wrong,
   frame by frame and track by track.
3. Leave with an annotated MP4 and a JSON report that match each other.

## Brand personality and tone

**Editorial night edition.** The app is set like a match report printed white
on slate, not a dashboard.

- Flat slate stock, never pure black. Warm paper-white text, never pure white.
  The warmth lives in the text, the coolness in the ground.
- Newsreader (serif) for headlines, figures and section marks. Archivo
  (grotesque) for UI, labels and body. No monospace as a "technical" signal.
- Hairline rules carry the structure. No cards, no nested containers, no drop
  shadows, no glassmorphism, no glow.
- Pale gold is the accent **and** it is the ball. The one warm mark on the page
  is always the thing you are looking for, so the accent is never spent on
  decoration. The two team inks (sky, coral) do the rest.
- Asymmetric composition - offset standfirst, one leading figure larger than
  the rest. Nothing is centered.
- Motion is one orchestrated arrival on load plus the possession bar wipe.
  Exponential easing, transform and opacity only, disabled under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Do not use cream paper with a rust or terracotta accent. That combination is
the house style of several well-known AI products, so it reads as generic
rather than as SportVision. A first attempt was rejected on those grounds.
Avoid warm off-white grounds and orange-red accents on this project.

The tone of the writing is plainspoken and non-promotional. It never claims
more certainty than the model has. When there is not enough evidence for
possession, the app says so rather than inventing a percentage.

## Technical constraints

- Streamlit. The stylesheet lives in `src/sportvision/theme.py` and targets
  Streamlit `data-testid` attributes, so it needs revisiting on a major
  Streamlit upgrade.
- Streamlit's own heading and paragraph rules outrank bare classes. Editorial
  elements are element-qualified (`h1.sv-headline`, `p.sv-note`) to win the
  cascade, and name their font face explicitly rather than relying on
  inheritance.
- `cli.py` passes the palette as `--theme.*` flags so base widget chrome
  matches the stylesheet for pip-installed users, who have no
  `.streamlit/config.toml`. Base is `dark`.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts with a local fallback stack. The app is
  otherwise offline-capable, so it must stay legible with no network.
- Text contrast meets WCAG AA against both slate tones (measured on #1D2027:
  fg 13.2, fg-2 8.6, fg-3 5.7, accent 8.4, team 1 6.0, team 2 4.8). Slate text
  on the team inks and on gold also passes, so labels sit on the color.
